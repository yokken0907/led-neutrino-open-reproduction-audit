\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We report a partial computational replication of the Brane--Dirac spectrum in Figure~1 of de Giorgi, Pasari, and Turner, together with a bounded audit of an attempted open reconstruction of their experiment-level exclusion contours. The successful replicated subset is restricted to the theoretical spectrum defined by Eqs.~(3.26)--(3.28). Starting from the published equations alone, without using the target authors' code, our previous LED kernel, Newtrinos, or a Daya Bay likelihood, we independently solved the infinite-tower eigenvalue equation for $m_D=1$ and $\mu_1=\{10,1,0.1\}$ and reproduced the corresponding mixing-normalisation structure. A second implementation based on finite KK sums at $N=5000,10000,20000$ followed by $1/N$ extrapolation agreed with the closed-form roots at all nine preregistered anchors, with a maximum mass difference of $1.29\times10^{-9}$. The truncated sums of $N_\lambda^2$ exceeded the target paper's $99\%$ numerical criterion for all three parameter choices. An initially failed raw equation-residual gate was subsequently shown to be ill-conditioned near cotangent poles: all 3552 plotted roots lie within the requested solver-coordinate tolerance under a derivative-scaled backward-error test, with a maximum normalized error of 0.2501; two worst cases were independently confirmed at 80-digit precision. We also tested four digitized Daya Bay points from Figure~5. None produced a unique pointwise replication candidate because the open comparator crossed the $\Delta\chi^2=4.605$ threshold multiple times within each digitization interval. Separately, the public Daya Bay covariance-fit reference was reproduced in the frozen environment to an absolute $\chi^2$ difference of $1.33\times10^{-11}$, while our earlier fixed-coordinate profiling route was shown to be non-equivalent to that public full fit. We therefore classify the work as a partial replication of Figure~1 only; no replication of the published exclusion contours, no new LED signal, and no error in the original analysis are claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Replication-status statement}
This manuscript uses the \texttt{[Re]} prefix only for the independently reproduced Figure~1 Brane--Dirac spectrum subset. ReScience C's current reviewer guidance defines a partial replication as a successful replication of a subset of the original results. The experiment-level Figure~5 exclusion contours remain unresolved in the open reconstruction reported here and are explicitly excluded from the successful-replication claim. We do not use $[\neg\mathrm{Re}]$ because the present audit does not establish that the original exclusion analysis is non-replicable or mistaken.
